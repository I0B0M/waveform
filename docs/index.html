01

How it works

Four beats, no windows

One hotkey, one floating pill, and your words at the cursor. There is no transcript window to copy out of and nothing to paste — the whole loop is speak, stop, keep typing.

01 / TAP

Press fn

The pill appears near your cursor and never steals focus. Hold fn for 2+ seconds instead and it's push-to-talk — release inserts immediately.

02 / TALK

Say it, badly

Live on-device transcription. The ribbons carry the signal: orange rides your voice, cyan stills if the recognizer stops understanding you.

03 / STOP

Just stop talking

Roughly 2.5 seconds of silence ends it on its own — brief pauses between sentences don't count. The hotkey always stops it immediately.

04 / LANDS

Text at the cursor

Cleaned and inserted wherever you were: Slack, VS Code, a browser field, Notes. Your final cursor wins, and the clipboard always holds a copy.

Hover the pill for the controls · ✕ discard · ✦ organize the text first (⌥-click builds a prompt) · ✓ insert exactly as spoken

02

Everything it does

All on-device

Grouped the way the app groups them — Speak, Teach it, AI mode — because the sidebar you'll use is organised the same way.

Speak

Filler removal

“um okay so basically…” becomes “Okay, so basically…”. Conservative rules that never rephrase you. Toggleable.

Self-correction

“…at 5, no wait, 6pm” resolves to “6pm”. You get to change your mind out loud, like a person.

Spoken formatting

“new paragraph”, “bullet point”, punctuation by name, and “delete that” — handled as commands, not typed out.

Streaming insertion

Words appear at your cursor live while you talk, then the cleaned version replaces them in place. Fields that can't take live writes fall back to inserting at the end.

Finish commands

End with “send it” to insert and press the app's send key. “Scratch that” throws the whole thing away. Only counts as its own sentence.

Undo the insertion

A menu-bar item that verifies the field still ends with what it wrote, in the same app, inside 15 seconds. It never blind-fires.

Teach it

It learns your vocabulary

Fix a word it typed — “prom” → “prompt”, “nestjs” → “NestJS” — and it notices, stores the correction, and stops making that mistake. Only the corrected word is kept, never what you were writing.

Personal dictionary

One term per line: names, jargon, acronyms. It biases recognition from the very next dictation.

Prompt library

Say //plan, //bug, //review, //ticket — or your own trigger — then ramble. Your words get poured into that shape.

Voice snippets

Say a trigger, get the stored text. Addresses, boilerplate, the paragraph you retype four times a week.

History & scratchpad

Local, capped, and clearable — stats and recent dictations come from a file on your own disk. Clearing it means clearing it.

Per-app style

Punctuation and casing adapt to where you're typing. Partial today — tone doesn't adapt yet, and the rules aren't editable.

AI mode

The ✦ button

While the pill is up, press ✦ instead of ✓ and the on-device model organizes your words before they land. ⌥-click builds a prompt instead of tidying a message. No speech recognition is involved, so it can't be misheard.

//commands

Say //better, //shorter, //professional or //prompt at the start and ramble — the local model restructures everything after it. Natural phrasing works too; the slash form never misfires.

Rewrite a selection

Select text anywhere, say a command with nothing after it, and it's rewritten in place. Double-tap fn for AI mode and reply straight to what you've highlighted.

Any brain you want

Apple Intelligence on your Mac by default and free. If you'd rather, point it at a local model or any provider's API on your own key — that path ships switched off.

If a rewrite fails a sanity check, your words are inserted exactly as spoken and the pill says why. It can never silently do nothing.

The window behind the pill

Menu-bar app

Most of the time Waveform is a menu-bar icon that shows live permission status — if something isn't working, that's the first place to look. Open the window and you get the dashboard: what you've dictated, what it has learned, and every switch.

Waveform

Home — stats, top apps and recent dictations, all read from a capped local file.

The whole privacy policy

Nothing leaves your Mac.

Speech recognition rides macOS 26's own on-device model. Cleanup is deterministic rules running locally. Rewriting uses Apple Intelligence, on your machine. There is no account to make, no telemetry to opt out of, and no server that could be breached, because there is no server.

Your history is a capped JSON file on your own disk. The dictionary only ever stores the corrected word — never the sentence you were writing when you fixed it. Both are visible in the app and clearing them means clearing them.

One exception, and it is yours to switch on: AI mode can be pointed at a local model or a cloud provider's API using your own key. It ships off. Turn it on and text goes where you told it to go — nowhere else.

04

How it's different

Honestly

Local dictation isn't rare any more. What's unusual here is zero setup: riding the speech model macOS already ships means no downloads, no keys, no runtime to install.

Waveform Cloud dictation Local, bring-your-own-model
Audio leaves your Mac Never Yes — that's the design No
Before first use Grant mic + accessibility Make an account, sign in Download a model, sometimes a runtime
On disk 2.6 MB An app plus a session App plus gigabytes of weights
Works offline Entirely No Yes
AI rewriting On-device, free, optional cloud on your key Server-side, on their key Varies — usually your local model
Cost Free, source on GitHub Subscription Mixed

The two right-hand columns describe categories, not one product. VoiceInk and Superwhisper are local too — they sit in the third column. Wispr Flow sits in the second and has no offline mode. For meeting and file transcription, MacWhisper owns that job; Waveform doesn't try to.

05

Install it in a minute

macOS 26 · Apple silicon

Just want to use it

  1. Download the DMG and drag Waveform to Applications.

  2. Right-click it → Open the first time. This build is signed with a locally-generated certificate rather than a paid Apple Developer ID, so macOS shows one warning it won't show again.

  3. Press the hotkey once and grant Microphone, then Accessibility — needed to insert text into other apps and for the fn hotkeys.

  4. The first dictation may download the system speech model once. macOS manages it; you don't.

Download the DMG

Build it yourself

Needs macOS 26 on Apple silicon and Xcode Command Line Tools — no Xcode. 

# one-time
xcode-select --install

git clone https://github.com/I0B0M/waveform
cd waveform
INSTALL=1 Scripts/build-app.sh
open /Applications/Waveform.app

The script makes a local “Waveform Dev” signing certificate on first run, so your permission grants survive rebuilds. Drop INSTALL=1 to keep the build local.

If macOS opens the emoji picker when you press fn, set System Settings → Keyboard → “Press 🌐 key to” → Do Nothing. Five other hotkey presets are in Settings, including double-tap Control.

What isn't built yet

Stated plainly

Roughly in the order it'd get added. Nothing here is hidden behind a “coming soon”.

  • NextEditable per-app rules. The bundle IDs are hardcoded today; that should be a table you control.
  • NextCustom hotkey recorder. Six fixed presets today, no free-form recorder.
  • SoonHealth tab. Last ten insertions with method and outcome, rewrite rejections with reasons, live permission state.
  • SoonGuided first run. Accessibility versus Input Monitoring confusion is the number-one support issue.
  • SoonAuto-update. The DMG has no update path — you'll be checking Releases for now.
  • NeverOther languages. English only, by choice.
  • NeverMeeting and file transcription. Different job, and MacWhisper already does it well.
07

Questions

Does any audio or text ever leave my Mac?
No. Recognition uses the on-device speech model in macOS 26, cleanup is local rules, and rewriting uses Apple Intelligence on your machine. The only path off the Mac is one you configure yourself in AI mode with your own API key — and it ships disabled.
Why do I have to right-click → Open?
The build is signed with a locally-generated certificate instead of a paid Apple Developer ID. macOS shows the warning once. Signing with a real Developer ID and notarising would remove it entirely — no code changes needed.
Why does it need Accessibility permission?
To write text into other applications, and to watch for the fn and double-tap-Control hotkeys. Without it, Waveform can hear you but can't put the words anywhere.
What are the requirements?
macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon. The AI features additionally need Apple Intelligence enabled — plain dictation works without it.
Does it slow my Mac down?
Idle cost is about zero: no audio engine, no timers and no animation while you're not dictating. The one exception is the double-tap-Control preset, which keeps a listen-only event tap alive — microseconds per keystroke.
Can it transcribe a meeting recording?
No, and it won't. Waveform is for speaking into the app you're already using. MacWhisper owns file and meeting transcription; that's a different job.
What if the AI rewrite gets it wrong?
If the model is unavailable or a rewrite fails its sanity check, your words are inserted exactly as spoken and the pill tells you why. It can never silently do nothing, and the clipboard always holds the text.
